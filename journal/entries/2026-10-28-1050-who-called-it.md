# Who called it

2026-10-28 10:50. Previous: [2026-10-28-0905-the-hunt-on-real-links.md](2026-10-28-0905-the-hunt-on-real-links.md)

## What happened

My newsletters now keep track of their own predictions. As each issue arrives, a cheap filter finds the sentences that look forward and name a time, and Haiku picks out the ones that could be checked later: who expects what, by when, in the newsletter's own words. Every morning at 7:30 the ones that came due are settled from what my later newsletters said: right, wrong or unclear, with the stories that show it. Library has a Predictions page where I can overrule any verdict, and a newsletter's page says how its own calls came out.

The archive held 172 at first. Many were not predictions at all but companies' own announced dates: Meta shipping frames on 13 October, OpenAI retiring models on the 23rd, Disney's CTO starting on the 2nd. They settle badly, since the date passes in silence or a loosely related story gets read as an answer. Asking Haiku to skip them did nothing. Asking it to label each one a forecast, a promise or a schedule, and dropping schedules in code, did. That left 117: 91 forecasts and 26 promises, 29 of them the newsletters' own calls.

Five were already due and the first settling took eight seconds and a cent: the Fed's September rate rise (called by the futures market, and by Fortune's writer), Bumble going private within the week, and Trump's expected talks with Iran, marked wrong because the stories show military preparations instead. That last one is the kind of call I should look at myself.

The rest come due slowly: the next on 2 November, then 28 at the end of December and 25 at the end of 2027.

## Why

Newsletters make confident calls and nobody keeps score. The archive can keep score for free: later issues report what happened. Starting now matters because predictions take months to come due.

## Where we are now

Milestone 7 is closed and on prod. The page has only been checked in mock mode. Settling reads my newsletters only; checking against the wider web waits on a search provider.

## Decisions

- Spot predictions in their own small call, after a regex finds forward-looking sentences, so the story index is left alone and the cost stays in pennies.
- A prediction is kept only if its quote is in the issue word for word.
- Label, then drop: forecasts and promises stay, schedules go.
- Right or wrong only from a story about the same thing; silence is unclear, tried again twice a fortnight apart.
- A newsletter's track record counts only its own calls, not the forecasts it reports.

## Direction changes

- Who called it (#24): was settling by web search, now settling from the archive first, the web later.
