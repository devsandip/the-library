# Check the source (#23)

A story against the page it links to: what the newsletter claimed, what the page says, a quote, and a verdict. The redesign's Source deep dive (SOURCE, "~10¢ · under a minute"), built as one page and one call.

## What it does

- **`GET` and `POST /nutgraf/api/issues/{id}/stories/{n}/source`** (`services/api/src/source.ts`): the POST starts a check and answers 202 `{ running }` at once; the GET answers `{ running }` while it runs, then the check kept for the story under `SOURCE#<issueId>#<nn>` (per story, since the link is the story's own), or `{ none }` with why the latest run kept nothing.
- **In the background (#127).** The check runs in the jobs Lambda (`services/api/src/jobs.ts`: 2 minutes, started by the API with an async invoke, no retries), so a check is not held to the API's 28 seconds and the hunt for the original (#128) can read several pages. The run is an item beside the check (sort key `RUN`): `running` from the POST, `none` with why or `failed` when the job keeps nothing; keeping a check deletes it in the same transaction. A second POST while a run is going answers that run. A run still `running` after 3 minutes (the job stops at 2) answers "The check did not finish. Try again." and can be started again.
- **Read the page safely** (`services/api/src/page.ts`). The link comes from a newsletter and the API Lambda can reach the internet, so:
  - http or https only, on ports 80 and 443, with no user name or password in the link;
  - every address the host resolves to must be public: not private, loopback, link-local (which includes the cloud metadata service), shared (CGNAT), multicast, documentation or reserved, in IPv4 or IPv6, including IPv4 written inside IPv6, 6to4 and NAT64. The check runs in the request's own DNS lookup, at connect time, so the answer cannot change after it; an address written as a number is checked before the request;
  - at most 3 redirects, each checked the same way; at most 2 MB (after decompression) and 8 seconds in all; only HTML, XHTML or plain text, so a PDF or a file says so.
- **The hunt for the original (#128).** In the job, the page the story links to is read with its links numbered ("[n]" after a link's text; relative links made whole against the page). One Haiku call per page (`find_original`, `services/api/src/hunt.ts`) says whether it is readable, whether it is the original for the story's claims (the paper or its abstract page, the company's own announcement, the filing, the repository, the dataset, the court document or the official statement) and, if not, up to three of its links most likely to lead there, each with what it is. The job follows them depth first (a page's best link before the page before's second), each read with the same safety rules, and stops at the original, at five pages (those that could not be read count), or after 70 seconds. Claude names links by number, so an address never comes from the model; no page is read twice (a `#fragment` is the same page). **One page per site (#135):** a link to a site already read gets no number, so Claude cannot pick it (a site is a host's last two names, so `www.` and subdomains count as one; three under a shared suffix such as co.uk, github.io or substack.com); nor does a link to a site that is never a source and cannot be read anyway (Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, Pinterest, Zendesk help desks, the app stores). Each numbered link shows its host ("[3 arxiv.org]"), so Claude can judge where it goes. The check then reads the original, or, when none was found, the first page that could be read, which is the story's own as before. When the original was reached from a report, the check is told so, for its note.
- **The path is kept** with the check: every page tried, in order, the story's link first, each with what the page before called it (`why`), what the original is (`what`) and why a page could not be read (`failed`); `original` says whether the page checked is the original. While the job runs, GET's `running` carries the pages tried so far and the one being read.
- **Check.** The page checked becomes text with the converter newsletters use (`htmlToCleanText`). One Claude call (Haiku 4.5, `check_source`) gets the story (headline, summary, names), the newsletter's passage about it (for a digest, the paragraph that shares the most words with the story, and its neighbours), and up to 12,000 characters of the page. It answers whether the page is an article at all, then up to five of the story's claims, each with what the page says, a quote of at most 30 words and a verdict: holds, goes further (a bigger number, a stronger word, something the page only suggests) or missing; an overall verdict; and a note when it matters, such as the page reporting another site's story.
- **Quotes checked.** A quote is kept only when the page holds it word for word, case, spacing and curly quotes aside. So a quote shown is always the page's own words; a claim whose quote did not match keeps its sentence without one.
- **No check when there is nothing to check.** No link: "This story has no source link to check." A page that cannot be read says why ("The source could not be read: the address is not a public one."). A page with almost no text, or one Claude says is not the article (a paywall, a sign-in, a home page): "The source could not be read as an article". Nothing is kept then.

## The tab (#116)

- **Phone:** the story sheet's Source tab, after Timeline and Takes (`/issue/:id/story/:n/source`). Idle, what it reads and costs (`The page it links to · ~1¢ · under half a minute`) with Run; "Checking · 0:02" while it runs; ✓ once kept. Kept: the overall verdict (Holds up in green, Goes further than the source in yellow, Not in the source in red, or Mixed), the page's host as a link, the note, then each claim with its verdict, what the page says, and the quote in italics. The receipt says it is kept for this story.
- **Mac:** the story index's key grid has a C row ("Check source", as the design draws it), C runs it, and the check shows compactly under the selected story.
- Built on the shared deep-dive store and frame (`web/src/timeline.ts`, `DeepPanel`), which now take what the status line says while running and whether a result is kept for the event or the story. The store reads a check that runs in the background again every 2 seconds until it settles (giving up after 4 minutes, with Try again), and a story opened while its check runs, on either device, shows it running.
- **The hunt on screen (#129).** While a check runs, the status line keeps "Checking · 0:07" and under it the pages tried so far list in order, each host linked, with "Reading arxiv.org…" for the page being read; on the Mac, one line of hosts. Kept, the line under the verdict says what it was checked against ("Checked against the original", "The story links to the original", "No original in 3 pages: checked against the story's link") with the page's host, then, when it followed links, the path: the story's link, each page with what the page before called it, the original in green with what it is, and pages that could not be read with why. Checks from before the hunt show the host alone, as before. The idle line reads "The page it links to and what it cites · ~2¢ · under half a minute", from the prod probes below (1 to 3.1 cents, 12 to 27 seconds).
- **Mock mode** checks in a stand-in background run of 5.5 seconds that hunts from the story's link through a wire story that answers 403 to the company's own post, a page every 1.5 seconds: one claim that holds, one the newsletter took further.

## Decisions

- **One page first, then the hunt.** The first version (#115) read only the page the story links to and said in its note when that page reported another source. The hunt (#128) follows those links: one small call per page to decide, then the same check as before against the page reached, so the check's prompt and its quote matching did not change.
- **Depth first, five pages, 70 seconds.** News pages tend to chain (newsletter, news report, wire story, original), so the best link is followed to its end before a page's second-best. Five pages and 70 seconds keep a run inside the job's 2 minutes with room for the check.
- **One page per site, enforced in code (#135).** The first probe showed the hunt wandering inside one site (a newsletter's other posts, four pages of one company's docs). The prompt said not to, but Claude saw only "[n]", not where a link went, so it could not tell. Now the code withholds those numbers and shows each link's host.
- **Some junk still costs a page.** When a page cites nothing off its site (Fortune's articles), Claude still names its likeliest link (a magazine order page, x.com) rather than none. That is one wasted read of a few seconds and about 0.3¢; adding more sites to a blocklist one by one would not end it.
- **Claude picks links by number.** As in the story index (#15), the model sees "[n]" and the code turns numbers back into the page's own links, so a page cannot make the job fetch an address it does not link to, and every fetch goes through the same public-address checks. A page could still steer which of its own links is followed; the worst it gets is a check against a page it chose, which the path shows.
- **Haiku, about half a cent.** The design costed Source at about 10¢ for an agent; one call over one page costs far less.
- **The safety lives in the connection,** not in a check before it: a check-then-fetch can be beaten by a DNS answer that changes between the two, so the address is checked as the socket connects.
- **Kept per story,** not per event like the timeline and takes: two newsletters on one event link to different pages.
- **A job, polled** (#127), not a longer request: HTTP API waits at most 30 seconds, and the hunt reads up to five pages of up to 8 seconds each with a Claude call between them. The web polls rather than holding a socket open, so a phone that sleeps mid-check finds the result when it wakes. The run is an item of its own beside the check, so starting or failing a run never rewrites a kept check.

## Probe (2026-10-27, prod, eight recent stories from eight newsletters, through the API Lambda)

Only the outcome of each was printed, not the stories or pages.

- **Five checked,** in 3.2 to 6.3 seconds for 0.4 to 0.7 cents: three hold up, two go further than their source; two to five claims each, and zero to three quotes per check survived the word-for-word match.
- **Three not read:** a 403 and a 429 (sites that turn away automated readers) and a paywall, each answered with the reason.
- So about five links in eight can be checked as they are. The reader announces itself honestly ("Nutgraf source check") rather than passing as a browser; sites that refuse it stay refused.

## Probes of the hunt (2026-10-28, prod, read-only: `scratch/source-hunt-probe.ts`, outcomes and hosts only)

Run from a laptop against prod's table, fetching the public pages and calling Haiku; nothing was kept.

- **Before #135, the newest story from each of ten newsletters.** Five links could not be read at all: The Ken, The Juggernaut and Business Insider link to their own paywalled pages, and WSJ What's News and TLDR IT answered 401. Of the five checked, only TLDR Dev's link was already an original (Anthropic's post); the other four wandered inside one site (Pragmatic Engineer and Fortune through their own posts, ByteByteGo through four pages of Apple's docs) and were checked against the newsletter's own page. 13 to 27 seconds, 1 to 3.1 cents.
- **With #135, the same ten.** ByteByteGo reached Apple's own machine learning post in four pages, by way of Apple's docs and Wikipedia; no hunt read two pages of one site. Seven links could not be read this time: the five above, the Pragmatic Engineer post (paywalled for subscribers) and x.com. 16 to 24 seconds, 1.3 to 2.6 cents.
- **With #135, digest stories, two per newsletter (the case the hunt is for).** Of twelve, five could be read and three of those reached an original: TLDR Dev from x.com to Anthropic's announcement, TLDR IT from a Betanews report to a copy of Microsoft's Message Center notice, and a TLDR Dev link that was already Mergify's own post. Fortune's two found none: its articles cite nothing off its own site. Seven were refused at the first page (WSJ's 401s, TLDR IT's 401, Pirate Wires' paywall and 429, The Ken's paywall). 12.5 to 19 seconds, 1 to 1.5 cents.
- **So:** when the first page can be read, the hunt reached an original in four of eight checks, three of them by following links. The limit is the first page: 14 of 22 links were refused, mostly newsletters linking to their own paywalled sites, which no hunt can get past.
